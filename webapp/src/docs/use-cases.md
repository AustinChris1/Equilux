# Use cases

Nine situations where Equilux helps, and who it helps in each. They all come back to one thing: **a pay figure people can trust without seeing anyone's pay**.

## 1. The yearly report of a large employer

**Who:** a company with 250 or more workers in the EU.

**When:** every year, from 7 June 2027 (on 2026 pay).

The company has to publish seven pay-gap figures. With Equilux it publishes them **with a proof** attached. Staff, journalists and the regulator can check the figures came from the full payroll, in seconds, without asking for a single salary.

## 2. The three-yearly report of a mid-size employer

**Who:** a company with 150 to 249 workers (and, from 2031, 100 to 149).

**When:** every three years.

Smaller companies have smaller HR teams. Equilux runs in the browser from a payroll export (Personio or DATEV CSV), so the report does not need a data team, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

## 3. The works council checks the payroll first

**Who:** the works council, the elected staff representatives (in Germany, the *Betriebsrat*).

**When:** before the report is calculated.

The council opens every sealed payroll row against the real payroll and approves it. Until it does, the report cannot even start. If a row is missing or wrong, the council sees it **before** any figure goes public, not after.

## 4. An employee checks they were counted

**Who:** any employee.

**When:** after the report is published.

From their payslip, an employee opens [the check page](/check) and sees, step by step: my row was committed, the council checked it, I confirmed it, and **I was counted once in each report**. They also see what women and men in **their own group** earn on average, which the law gives them a right to know (Article 7). No wallet, no fee, nothing leaves their browser.

## 5. The regulator verifies a filed report

**Who:** the national body that receives the reports (a labour inspectorate or equality body).

**When:** when a report is filed, or when a complaint comes in.

Instead of asking the company for its payroll, the regulator opens [the verify page](/verify), enters the company's contract address, and reads the proven figures straight from the chain. It can also load the company's filing and check each figure against the chain.

## 6. A gap of 5% or more is flagged

**Who:** employer, works council, regulator.

**When:** a group of comparable workers shows a gap of 5% or more.

The law then asks for a **joint pay assessment** if the gap is not explained by objective, gender-neutral reasons (Article 10). Equilux proves the gap and **flags** it. Deciding whether it is justified stays a human decision, made by people who can now trust the number they are discussing.

## 7. A dispute about equal pay

**Who:** the employer and its lawyers.

**When:** an employee claims they are paid less for equal work.

Under the Directive, the **employer must show** there was no discrimination (Article 18). A report whose figures are proven over the complete, staff-confirmed payroll is evidence the employer can point to, which a self-made spreadsheet is not.

## 8. A payroll company offers it to its clients

**Who:** payroll providers and HR software companies.

**When:** every reporting season, for every client in scope.

The payroll provider already holds the payroll. It seals the rows it already has, and its clients get a report anyone can check. For the provider, it is a feature its clients will need from 2027.

## 9. A fair-pay certificate backed by proof

**Who:** certifiers that award "fair pay" labels, and the companies that seek them.

**When:** at certification and renewal.

Today a certificate rests on data the company hands over. With Equilux, the certifier can rely on figures that are **proven** to come from the complete payroll, and the company can show its certificate is backed by more than its own word.

---

> **What Equilux does not do.** It does not decide whether a gap is fair, and it does not hide salaries from HR, who already hold the payroll. It hides them from everyone else, and it stops the report being bent.
