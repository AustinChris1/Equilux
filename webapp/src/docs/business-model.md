# Business model

Who needs Equilux, who would pay for it, and how. Everything on this page about buyers and prices is a **plan to test**, based on desk research. No company uses or pays for Equilux yet.

## The market the law creates

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who must report** | Every employer with 100+ workers in the EU, in stages: 250+ yearly from June 2027, 150–249 every three years from June 2027, 100–149 from 2031 |
| **How many large employers** | About **53,000** EU businesses employ 250 or more people (Eurostat, 2023). That is 0.2% of firms, but over a third of all business jobs. |
| **Where it applies** | Any employer with staff in the EU, wherever it is based |
| **What is at stake** | Fines set by each country; a joint pay assessment when a group's gap is 5% or more and unexplained; and, in a dispute, the employer must prove it did not discriminate |

## Who pays

The **employer** has the legal duty, so the employer pays. The question is how Equilux reaches it. Three routes, most likely first:

### 1. Through the tools that already make the report

Pay-transparency software and payroll providers already calculate the report for their clients. Equilux adds one thing they can't: **proof** that the report covers the whole payroll. They add it to their product and resell it.

### 2. Directly, to reduce legal risk

In an equal-pay dispute, the employer must prove there was no discrimination. A report proven over the complete, staff-confirmed payroll is stronger evidence than a spreadsheet. Employment lawyers and compliance teams are the buyers here.

### 3. Pulled in by works councils and certifiers

Works councils rarely have a budget, but they can ask their employer to use a report they can check. Fair-pay certifiers can back their certificate with proof. Neither pays much directly; both create demand.

## How it would be priced

**Per employee, per year**, collected through the partner in route 1.

Why per employee: the work grows with headcount, almost one transaction per person. A report for 250 people takes about 346 transactions, 250 of them the employees' own confirmations. So the cost of running Equilux scales with company size, and the price should too.

No price is set. Fees on Midnight's main network are not known yet, and no buyer has been asked. The first pilots will ask: *what would this have to cost to be worth it?*

## Who else is in the market

Pay-equity tools already exist (for example Syndio, Trusaic and Figures). They analyse pay and help write the report, and they sell to mid-size and large companies by quote.

None of them makes the report **checkable by others without showing salaries**. That is the gap Equilux fills, so it can sit **beside** them as a layer, not against them. Route 1 above is exactly that.

## Where we are, honestly

- **Product:** live, free, and works today for up to 512 people per report.
- **Customers:** none yet. **Revenue:** none.
- **Pilots:** open. Employers, works councils and payroll providers can [ask for one](/#pilot).
- **Outreach:** on 3 November 2026 we emailed five organisations in Germany that work on this report: two works-council trainers, an equal-pay consultancy, a pay-transparency software company and a fair-pay certifier. No replies yet; every answer, including silence, will be reported here as it is.

## What would help next

- **An introduction** to one payroll or HR software company for a first real pilot.
- **Fee sponsorship on Midnight**, so the employer can pay every employee's small fee and nobody has to hold a token.
- **Fee sponsorship built into Midnight wallets**, so an employer can pay for a whole team of walletless confirmations in one approval instead of one per person.
